ABSTRACT
Battery-free embedded systems powered by ambient energy harvesting have emerged as a transformative paradigm for sustainable Internet of Things (IoT) deployments. The period spanning 2025 to 2026 marks a critical inflection point, with research transitioning from feasibility demonstrations to scalable, reliable, and industry-ready implementations. This review paper examines six seminal research contributions that represent the forefront of battery-free embedded system development: analytical energy modeling for batteryless IoT sensors, cost-effective design methodologies for intermittent wireless communication, performance evaluation platforms for battery-less systems, harvesting-aware neural architecture search for intermittent TinyML, UAV-powered RF energy-harvesting sensor networks for precision agriculture, and comprehensive circuit-level surveys of intermittent computing architectures. We provide a structured analysis of each contribution, identify critical research gaps including the lack of unified benchmarking frameworks, limited multi-source energy harvesting integration, insufficient edge AI adaptation for intermittent operation, and inadequate standardization of power management protocols. Our findings suggest that future research must prioritize cross-platform interoperability, energy-aware machine learning co-design, and robust reliability frameworks to accelerate the deployment of maintenance-free, sustainable embedded systems.
Keywords: Battery-Free Embedded Systems, Energy Harvesting, Intermittent Computing, IoT, TinyML, Power Management, Wireless Sensor Networks, Research Gaps
I. INTRODUCTION
The proliferation of Internet of Things (IoT) devices across industrial, agricultural, environmental, and healthcare domains has been fundamentally constrained by a persistent challenge: the reliance on batteries as primary power sources. Batteries impose significant limitations including periodic replacement costs, environmental hazards from chemical waste, restricted operational lifespans in extreme environments, and maintenance overhead that undermines the scalability of large-scale sensor deployments. These constraints have motivated a growing body of research into battery-free embedded systems that harvest ambient energy from sources such as solar radiation, radio-frequency (RF) signals, thermal gradients, and mechanical vibrations.
The period from 2025 to 2026 represents a pivotal transition in this field. Research has progressed from small-scale feasibility studies to addressing the fundamental challenges of reliability, scalability, and real-world deployment. Key advancements include sophisticated analytical models for predicting batteryless sensor behavior under varying environmental conditions [1], cost-effective design methodologies that optimize energy storage and power management for intermittent wireless communication [2], and standardized evaluation platforms that enable fair and repeatable benchmarking of battery-less IoT systems [3]. Simultaneously, the convergence of energy harvesting with edge artificial intelligence has given rise to the emerging paradigm of Intermittent TinyML, where neural network architectures are specifically designed to operate under intermittent power supply [4].
Furthermore, novel application paradigms have emerged, such as UAV-powered RF energy-harvesting sensor networks that enable battery-free precision agriculture [5], while comprehensive surveys have systematically catalogued the circuit-level innovations driving intermittent computing architectures [6]. These developments collectively signal that battery-free embedded systems are transitioning from academic curiosities to practical, deployable technologies.
This review paper provides a comprehensive examination of six landmark research contributions published between 2024 and 2026, analyzing their methodologies, key innovations, and experimental results. Furthermore, we identify and highlight critical research gaps that remain unaddressed, offering directions for future investigation. The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: Section II presents the reviewed literature; Section III provides a comparative analysis; Section IV identifies research gaps; Section V discusses implications and future directions; and Section VI concludes the paper.
II. LITERATURE REVIEW
A. Analytical Modeling of Batteryless IoT Sensors Powered by Ambient Energy Harvesting
Fernández Landivar et al. (arXiv:2507.20952, July 2025) present a comprehensive mathematical framework for characterizing the energy dynamics of batteryless IoT sensor nodes [1]. The model addresses both energy harvesting and consumption phases, explicitly incorporating power management tasks that are often overlooked in existing literature. The Energy Harvesting Unit (EHU)—comprising the ambient energy harvester, power management module, and supercapacitor-based energy buffer—is modeled in conjunction with the Circuit Load (CL) to provide precise estimation of device behavior, including voltage variations in the energy buffer across various environmental conditions.
A key strength of this work is its generalizability: the analytical framework is designed to be applicable to a wide range of IoT devices rather than being tied to a specific hardware platform. The authors validated their model against a prototype batteryless IoT node by conducting experiments under three distinct illumination scenarios, demonstrating a strong correlation between predicted and measured supercapacitor voltage profiles. The model supports the design and optimization of intelligent power management units intended to maximize harvested energy despite fluctuating environmental inputs, providing engineers with a tool for energy profiling and performance optimization prior to physical deployment.
B. Designing Cost-Effective Battery-Less Energy Harvesting for Intermittent Wireless Communication
Published in IEEE Access (Volume 13, pp. 157044–157058, January 2025), this paper proposes a cost-effective design methodology for battery-less IoT devices that must support reliable wireless communication under intermittent energy availability [2]. The authors develop comprehensive energy and timing models to optimize the sizing of energy harvesting and short-term energy storage components, particularly capacitors, enabling designers to identify optimal configurations that meet specific wireless performance requirements without traditional batteries.
The central innovation is a duty-based Dynamic Power Management (DPM) scheme that significantly outperforms traditional threshold-based approaches. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed DPM scheme extends device operation time by 9.1% to 90.0% depending on environmental and weather conditions. The power management architecture is specifically tailored to support improved Quality of Service (QoS), targeting data transmission rates and communication frequency. This work bridges the gap between theoretical energy harvesting models and practical deployment requirements, providing a systematic framework for engineers to design battery-less wireless communication systems with predictable performance guarantees.
C. EStacker: Explaining Battery-Less IoT System Performance with Energy Stacks
Liedtke et al. present EStacker in ACM Transactions on Embedded Computing Systems (Volume 25, Issue 1, 2026), a specialized evaluation platform for battery-less IoT systems [3]. EStacker addresses the critical challenge of fair and repeatable performance evaluation by generating 'energy stacks'—detailed breakdowns of an application's total energy consumption across specific hardware components and application activities. This granular analysis allows developers to understand precisely where harvested energy is being consumed, enabling targeted optimization of both hardware and software components.
A significant technical contribution is the ST-SP (Scaling Time–Scaling Power) optimization technique, which reduces evaluation time by an average of 6.3× while maintaining the temporal behavior of the system with high accuracy (average throughput error of only 7.7%). This speedup is crucial for exploring large design spaces, such as harvester and energy storage sizing, that would otherwise require prohibitively long evaluation cycles. EStacker ensures that each application and configuration is tested under identical energy conditions, establishing a foundation for standardized benchmarking in the battery-less IoT community.
D. HANNA: Harvesting-Aware Neural Network Architecture Search
Sahu, Deep, and Duwe introduce HANNA at the IEEE International Performance, Computing, and Communications Conference (IPCCC 2024), presenting an energy-harvesting-aware, one-shot differentiable Neural Architecture Search (NAS) method specifically designed for batteryless intermittent devices [4]. Unlike standard hardware-aware NAS approaches that primarily optimize for latency and accuracy, HANNA incorporates the energy-harvesting dynamics of the deployment environment into the architecture search process, producing adaptive deep neural network (DNN) inference architectures suitable for intermittent computing environments.
The key insight is that optimal neural network topologies for batteryless devices are fundamentally different from those designed for continuous power: they must account for power interruptions, checkpointing overhead, and variable energy availability. HANNA's differentiable NAS formulation enables efficient exploration of this constrained design space, producing architectures that can complete inference tasks within the energy budget of a single harvesting cycle. This work represents one of the earliest concrete contributions to the emerging field of Intermittent TinyML, where machine learning workloads are adapted to the unique constraints of energy-harvesting embedded systems.
E. Autonomous Agricultural Monitoring with Aerial Drones and RF Energy-Harvesting Sensor Tags
Kudyba and Sun (arXiv:2502.16028, February 2025) explore a cost-effective and sustainable approach to precision agriculture by replacing traditional battery-powered sensors with battery-less RF energy-harvesting sensor tags powered by UAV-emitted beacon signals [5]. The system architecture employs unmanned aerial vehicles equipped with specialized wireless payloads that broadcast RF signals. These signals are harvested by passive sensor tags to power environmental measurements (temperature, humidity) and data transmission back to the drone, enabling fully autonomous data collection without any ground-level power infrastructure.
The researchers conducted experimental trials at AERPAW, an NSF-funded wireless aerial drone research platform. Ground-based testing successfully demonstrated the concept, while airborne trials identified challenges related to wireless interference that affected data detection reliability. The authors propose that unifying receiver hardware to reduce weight and interference could improve system robustness. This work demonstrates a compelling application paradigm for battery-free embedded systems in agriculture, highlighting both the potential and the practical engineering challenges of deploying RF-powered sensor networks at scale.
F. Batteryless Systems for IoT: A Survey of Circuit and System Design
Presented at the IEEE Midwest Symposium on Circuits and Systems (MWSCAS) 2025, this comprehensive survey systematically catalogues the circuit-level and system-level innovations driving the development of batteryless IoT systems [6]. The survey covers the full technology stack, including energy transducer design, power management integrated circuits (PMICs), energy storage technologies (supercapacitors, thin-film batteries), non-volatile memory architectures for state preservation during power failures, and intermittent computing execution models.
The paper provides a taxonomy of intermittent computing approaches, distinguishing between task-based execution models (where computation is structured into atomic tasks that can be completed within a single energy cycle) and checkpoint-based approaches (where program state is periodically saved to non-volatile memory). The survey identifies key design trade-offs including harvester efficiency versus cost, storage capacity versus leakage, and computational progress versus energy overhead, providing a roadmap for researchers and practitioners entering the field of battery-free embedded system design.

The six reviewed papers collectively represent three major research thrusts in contemporary battery-free embedded system development: (1) theoretical modeling and design methodology (Papers [1] and [2]), (2) evaluation, benchmarking, and architecture innovation (Papers [3] and [4]), and (3) application-driven system integration and comprehensive technology surveys (Papers [5] and [6]). A notable convergence across all works is the shift from demonstrating the mere feasibility of batteryless operation to addressing practical deployment challenges including reliability, predictability, and scalability.
The modeling contributions of Fernández Landivar et al. [1] and the design methodology of the IEEE Access paper [2] are complementary: while [1] provides the analytical foundation for understanding energy dynamics, [2] translates these insights into actionable design rules for wireless communication systems. Similarly, EStacker [3] provides the evaluation infrastructure needed to validate designs produced by approaches like HANNA [4], creating a natural toolchain for battery-free system development. The application-focused work of Kudyba and Sun [5] demonstrates real-world deployment challenges that motivate the theoretical and systems-level innovations in the other papers, while the MWSCAS survey [6] provides the comprehensive technology landscape that contextualizes all contributions.
IV. IDENTIFIED RESEARCH GAPS
Despite the substantial progress documented in the reviewed literature, several critical research gaps remain that warrant urgent investigation. These gaps are highlighted below:
A. Lack of Unified Benchmarking and Evaluation Standards
While EStacker [3] represents a significant step toward standardized evaluation, there is no universally accepted benchmarking framework or standard dataset for comparing battery-free embedded system performance across different hardware platforms, energy harvesting sources, and application workloads. Current evaluations are conducted using proprietary setups with non-reproducible environmental conditions, making cross-study comparisons extremely difficult. A community-wide effort to establish reference implementations, standard energy traces, and common performance metrics (e.g., energy-per-task, recovery latency, throughput under intermittence) is urgently needed.
B. Insufficient Multi-Source Energy Harvesting Integration
Most reviewed works focus on single energy harvesting modalities (solar in [1], RF in [5]), yet robust methodologies for dynamically integrating and managing multiple concurrent energy sources (solar, RF, thermal, kinetic) in a unified power management framework remain largely unexplored. Real-world deployment environments present diverse and time-varying energy availability patterns. Hybrid harvesting architectures that can seamlessly switch between or combine multiple sources to maximize energy availability and minimize downtime represent a critical unmet research need for achieving truly maintenance-free operation.
C. Limited Edge AI and TinyML Adaptation for Intermittent Operation
HANNA [4] represents a pioneering contribution to intermittent TinyML, however the broader adaptation of deep learning and on-device inference techniques for intermittent, energy-harvesting embedded systems remains severely underdeveloped. Critical challenges include developing efficient checkpointing strategies for neural network inference that minimize energy overhead, creating models resilient to partial execution across power cycles, and designing energy-aware training methodologies. The emerging paradigm of compute-in-memory (CiM) architectures shows promise but lacks integration with intermittent computing frameworks.
D. Inadequate Standardization of Power Management Protocols
The duty-based DPM scheme in [2] and the energy modeling in [1] represent isolated solutions, but there is no standardized protocol or middleware layer for power management across heterogeneous battery-free IoT devices. Without standardization, each device family requires custom power management logic, hindering interoperability and increasing development costs. An open, modular power management middleware—analogous to communication protocol stacks—that abstracts energy harvesting, storage, and consumption management would significantly accelerate ecosystem growth.
E. Reliability and Long-Term Durability in Harsh Environments
While battery-free systems are often positioned for deployment in harsh industrial or agricultural environments, there is insufficient research on the long-term reliability, degradation behavior, and functional safety of batteryless systems under sustained exposure to extreme temperatures, humidity, vibration, and electromagnetic interference. The RF interference challenges observed in [5]'s airborne trials exemplify this gap. Comprehensive reliability studies spanning months or years of continuous deployment, coupled with formal safety certification frameworks for battery-free devices in safety-critical applications (e.g., industrial monitoring, medical devices), are essential for industry adoption.
F. Scalability of Battery-Free Wireless Sensor Networks
Individual device-level innovations dominate the current literature, but the scalability challenges of deploying hundreds or thousands of battery-free nodes in dense wireless sensor networks—including network-level energy management, interference mitigation, and data aggregation under intermittent connectivity—are largely unaddressed. As battery-free IoT transitions from single-node prototypes to large-scale deployments, network-level protocols that account for the intermittent and heterogeneous availability of individual nodes will be critical. Current MAC protocols and routing algorithms are designed for continuously powered devices and perform poorly under intermittent conditions.
V. DISCUSSION AND FUTURE DIRECTIONS
The research landscape of battery-free embedded systems in 2025–2026 reveals a field at a critical maturation point. The convergence of advanced energy modeling [1], practical design methodologies [2], standardized evaluation platforms [3], AI-aware architecture design [4], novel application paradigms [5], and comprehensive technology surveys [6] suggests that the foundational building blocks for scalable battery-free IoT deployments are falling into place. However, significant integration challenges remain.
Several promising future directions emerge from this review: (1) Development of a unified, open-source benchmarking ecosystem for battery-free systems, incorporating standardized energy traces, reference hardware platforms, and common performance metrics; (2) Research into adaptive multi-source energy harvesting with intelligent source selection based on real-time environmental sensing; (3) Co-design of TinyML models and intermittent computing hardware that jointly optimize inference accuracy, energy efficiency, and resilience to power interruptions; (4) Creation of standardized power management middleware that enables plug-and-play integration of diverse energy harvesting and storage components; (5) Long-term field studies establishing reliability baselines and degradation models for battery-free devices under real-world conditions; and (6) Network-level protocol design that inherently accounts for intermittent node availability, enabling scalable deployments of battery-free wireless sensor networks.
Furthermore, the growing commercial interest in maintenance-free IoT—driven by corporate sustainability mandates and the impracticality of battery replacement in large-scale deployments—is creating strong industry pull for the technologies reviewed here. The maturation of indoor photovoltaics, ultra-low-power silicon, and advanced power management ICs in 2025–2026 suggests that battery-free embedded systems are approaching a tipping point where academic innovations can be translated into commercially viable products at scale.
VI. CONCLUSION
This review paper has examined six seminal research contributions that define the state-of-the-art in battery-free embedded system development during 2025–2026. From Fernández Landivar et al.'s analytical modeling framework for batteryless IoT sensors [1], to cost-effective design methodologies for intermittent wireless communication [2], EStacker's standardized evaluation platform [3], HANNA's harvesting-aware neural architecture search [4], Kudyba and Sun's UAV-powered agricultural sensing [5], and the comprehensive MWSCAS circuit design survey [6], these works collectively represent a paradigm shift from proof-of-concept demonstrations to deployment-ready technologies.
However, significant research gaps persist, particularly in unified benchmarking standards, multi-source energy harvesting integration, TinyML adaptation for intermittent operation, power management protocol standardization, long-term reliability validation, and network scalability. Addressing these gaps will be essential for realizing the vision of truly maintenance-free, sustainable IoT ecosystems. We hope this review serves as a useful reference for researchers and practitioners working at the frontier of battery-free embedded system development, particularly those in the computer engineering community seeking to contribute to this rapidly evolving field.
